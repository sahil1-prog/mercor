Problem: A clinical pharmacology team needs day-14 steady-state exposure predictions to select a phase-2 dose from a once-daily multiple-ascending-dose (MAD) study. The test compound induces its own clearance through an enzyme turnover process regulated by plasma concentrations. Systemic clearance therefore increases as dosing continues, rises higher when a patient steps up in dose, and declines during washout. In this study design, roughly 70% of participants double their daily dose on day 8. However, pharmacokinetic blood sampling in the prospective cohort occurs solely on days 1, 4, and 7—entirely before that dose escalation. Predicting day-14 exposure is thus a genuine extrapolation across both dose and time. Moreover, the critical trough concentrations that carry the autoinduction signal are largely below the assay's lower limit of quantification (LLOQ = 2.50 ng/mL). Discarding below-quantification (BLQ) samples, assuming static clearance, or expecting dose-linear exposure severely distorts predictions.

Deliverable: An offline executable Python script placed at /app/submission/predict.py. The environment provides historical clinical cohorts (a rich cohort with full day-14 concentration profiles and a sparse cohort matching the target sampling schedule), a simple non-compartmental baseline script, and an internal memo containing faulty pharmacokinetic assumptions. The script reads these files along with prospective cohort data and writes an output CSV containing subject_id, AUC_ss, Cmax_ss, and Ctrough_ss for day 14. Constraints: no internet access, maximum runtime of 120 seconds wall-clock (with up to 180 seconds allowed on the verifier host), 4 CPUs, and 8 GB RAM. The program must accept two optional flags:

--horizon-h H : Duration of the forecast evaluation interval in hours across [t14, t14 + H), where t14 is the day-14 dose time (default 24.0, valid for 0 < H <= 168.0). AUC, Cmax, and Ctrough are all calculated across this specific interval, with Ctrough defined as the concentration at t14 + H.

--estimator {map, population} : Inference approach used for individual predictions (default map: empirical-Bayes MAP estimates given a population model). Under population mode, individual random effects are omitted; predictions derive purely from the subject's dosing history and demographic covariates, staying completely invariant to sample concentrations, sampling times, censor indicators, and sample counts. Both options must finish within the runtime ceiling and emit valid CSV files.

Covariates fallback: Clinical study tables frequently contain missing entries. When a subject lacks a record in covariates.csv or has a blank/unparseable body weight, the script must default that field to 70 kg; an invalid or missing age defaults to 45 years; and a missing or non-standard sex defaults to 'M'. Imputation must operate independently per field without halting execution or affecting predictions for other participants.

Scoring: Evaluated on an unseen test cohort of roughly 150 participants whose true day-14 exposure values are generated from latent individual pharmacokinetic parameters. The composite loss is raw = 0.5*median(r_AUCss) + 0.3*median(r_Cmaxss) + 0.2*median(r_Ctroughss), where r = |pred - true| / true. The continuous reward is clip(1 - raw/0.5, 0, 1). Submissions pass (score 1) if reward >= 0.50 (raw <= 0.25). Grader checks also verify CSV formatting, strict runtime enforcement, correct window scaling under --horizon-h across default and four test horizons (1.05 h, 6.05 h, 24.05 h, 47.95 h), estimator sample-invariance properties, and missing covariate handling. Timeouts, malformed tables, or runtime errors score 0.

Why it's hard: The critical autoinduction signal is hidden in censored trough measurements from sparse sampling schedules. A viable solution requires building a nonlinear population PK model with time-varying clearance (such as turnover-driven enzyme kinetics where clearance asymptotically rises under drug exposure) and integrating an exact censored-data likelihood (Beal's M3 method, which evaluates BLQ points as P(y < LLOQ) rather than discarding them). Additionally, stable empirical-Bayes MAP individualization requires well-tuned priors to avoid overfitting subjects with few quantifiable samples. Because standard NLME packages (like NONMEM or Monolix) are unavailable, all ODE solvers, likelihood computations, and optimizers must be implemented from scratch and execute in under 120 seconds on 4 CPU cores. Standard shortcuts fail: omitting BLQ records biases clearance downward, and constant-clearance assumptions overpredict day-14 concentrations.

Baseline and headroom (measured, local Docker): On the evaluation container, the naive non-compartmental script yields raw = 1.768 (reward 0.0); multivariable linear regression on dose and demographics scores raw = 0.514 (reward 0.0). Using true generating parameters, a model omitting autoinduction scores 0.0, population typical values alone score 0.487, and time-only induction that ignores plasma concentration achieves 0.638 at 24 h but drops to 0.511 on the 47.95 h washout window. The reference solution (M3 censored-likelihood population fit with enzyme autoinduction and MAP individualization) achieves raw = 0.109 (reward 0.783) at 24 h and 0.667 at 47.95 h, passing all 23 grader tests.

Research: Relevant literature includes nonlinear mixed-effects modeling methods (FOCE/SAEM), Beal (2001) strategies for handling bioanalytical censoring (M1 to M7), and published autoinduction turnover formulations (e.g. for carbamazepine and rifampicin). The solver must construct a parsimonious structural model suited to sparse data, code the censored likelihood and optimizer, handle subjects with all-BLQ records, calibrate priors from historical data, maintain numerical stability across both estimators, and compute steady-state metrics across arbitrary time horizons.

Resources and privacy: Operates on 4 CPUs, 8 GB RAM, and standard scientific Python (numpy, scipy, pandas) without GPUs. Visible training cohorts are simulated from an oral one-compartment disposition model featuring absorption lag, concentration-dependent enzyme turnover, dose titration, residual assay variance, and LLOQ censoring. The hidden evaluation cohort is generated with an independent seed (8675309) and retained solely inside the grader container, keeping test labels inaccessible to the agent.
